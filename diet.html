Nutrition

FUEL YOUR GAME

What you eat decides how you train, how you recover and how you feel in the 80th minute. Eat for performance, not just for hunger.

See matchday meals

Matchday

Before, During, After

3–4 Hours Before

Up

Slow-release carbs with a little protein.

Keep it simple and familiar.

  • Pasta or rice with chicken
  • Banana or toast with honey
  • Water, no sugary drinks

During

Stay Topped Up

Small, regular sips. Replace what you sweat out.

  • Water throughout
  • Isotonic drink for long games
  • Orange slices at half-time

Within 60 Minutes After

Recover

Protein rebuilds muscle and carbs refill your energy.

  • Chicken wrap or tuna sandwich
  • Chocolate milk or yoghurt
  • Fruit and a big glass of water

Example Only

A Sample Training Day

Meal What Why
Breakfast Oats, 2 eggs, fruit Steady energy and protein
Lunch Chicken, rice, vegetables Carbs to train, protein to repair
Snack Yoghurt, fruit, handful of nuts Keeps energy up before training
Dinner Fish, potatoes, green vegetables Recovery and healthy fats
Evening Glass of milk or a banana Light and sleep-friendly

Hydration Guide

  • Sip water through the whole day, not just at training
  • Pale yellow urine means you are well hydrated
  • Drink extra on hot days and after sweaty sessions
  • Bring your own bottle to every session

Foods to Limit

  • Sugary drinks and energy drinks
  • Fast food and deep-fried meals
  • Sweets and crisps, especially before matches
  • Skipping breakfast on training days

This is general guidance for young players. For a personal plan, speak to your coach, club or a registered sports dietitian.
